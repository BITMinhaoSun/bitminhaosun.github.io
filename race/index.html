Abstract

Training multimodal search agents requires tasks that make visually grounded search necessary for determining the answer. Textual identity clues and unused target evidence allow shortcuts in synthetic questions, weakening supervision for visual search and evidence integration.

We propose RACE, an image-conditioned task synthesis framework that constructs dependencies from images to targets and from target-specific evidence to answers.

Read the full abstractShow less

Referential Abstraction (RA) replaces identity-revealing text with specific visual references or ambiguous relational references resolved by searching among depicted candidates. Compositional Evidence (CE) links retrieved facts about multiple entities in one image or entities from different images into joint questions whose answers require every designated contribution. Both operations also apply to images found during search, allowing new visual observations to determine subsequent search targets or supply answer evidence.

Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-27B on only 12K teacher-generated trajectories from RACE raises average accuracy across ten multimodal benchmarks from 53.5 to 56.0, a relative improvement of approximately 4.7% over the matched base agent. Our code, data, and model will be publicly available.

01The problem

Two shortcuts let a solver skip visual search

A task needs two links: from the image to each target, and from every target's evidence to the answer. Text that names the target breaks the first. A question that mentions two targets but needs only one target's evidence breaks the second.

02Method

Two operations make visually grounded search necessary

RACE synthesizes tasks from real images: social-media photographs, Bilibili video frames and MovieNet film imagery. Referential Abstraction ties target resolution to the image. Compositional Evidence makes each selected target's evidence necessary for the joint answer. A teacher then solves each task from its images and question alone.

Operation 1

Referential Abstraction

Replace identity-revealing text with a reference that is resolved among the candidates depicted in the image.

M(V, ρ; F) = {e ∈ C(V, ν) : φ(e; Fe) = 1} = {e*} M(V, ρ; F) = {e ∈ C(V, ν) : φ(e; Fe) = 1} = {e*}

Both forms of reference require a unique match e* among the candidates C(V, ν) that show the visible cues ν.

Specific visual references
Appearance, position, order, spatial relations or occlusion locate the target before retrieval: the third person from the left, the person partly hidden behind the post.
Ambiguous relational references
Several candidates fit the visible cues, and a retrieved relation selects one: the man in the image who appeared in a crime film in year y. Construction searches every candidate, supports one match and rules out all alternatives; unresolved cases are rejected.

Operation 2

Compositional Evidence

Link retrieved facts about multiple targets, in one image or across images, into a joint question whose answer needs every contribution.

a = c(z1, …, zk), k ≥ 2

Joint sufficiencyYc([k]) = {a}

Individual necessity|Yc([k] \ {j})| > 1 for every j

All designated facts determine one answer; removing any one leaves it undetermined.

Within one image
Multiple grounded targets in the same image, for example two actors in one film still.
Across images
One grounded target per image; removing an image's contributions must also leave the answer undetermined.
Construction detailsApplying the operations during search · construction feedback · teacher trajectories and SFT

Applying the operations during search

A retrieved image J can supply answer evidence, a = v(J), or determine the next search target. Referential Abstraction then applies again inside the retrieved scene:

W → retrieve → J → ρW′ → W′ → search → z(W′)

As requirements expand, an earlier answer can become an intermediate fact.

Construction feedback

Static checks reject identity or answer leakage. A question-only probe then searches from the question alone, and a judge flags answer recovery, identity recovery or substantial candidate narrowing. Detected failures guide changes to the references, evidence or relation. A task is kept only when

gref(T) = gsup(T) = gdep(T) = 1, b(T) = 0

Reference grounding, answer support, evidence and image necessity, and no detected shortcut.

Teacher trajectories and SFT

Qwen3.7-Plus solves each retained task with search tools, receiving only its images and question. Protocol-valid trajectories whose final answers are judged correct are kept, and Qwen3.5-27B is trained on 12K of them in one SFT stage.

The loss covers assistant tool calls and responses; tool observations provide context.

03Results

Better on nine of ten benchmarks, from only 12K trajectories

The base agent and RACE-27B share the Qwen3.5-27B backbone, test sets, tools, decoding settings, inference budgets and scoring; each score is the mean of four sampled runs per question. SFT improves all four primary benchmarks by 2.2–5.0 points, 3.2 on average, and raises accuracy on nine of the ten benchmarks.

Training-data comparison

Less data, better supervision

With the same Qwen3.5-27B backbone, SFT on only 12K RACE trajectories exceeds SFT on 28K open-source trajectories, drawn from OpenSearch-VL, Vision-DeepResearch and REDSearcher-MM, by 0.4–3.0 points on every primary benchmark.

The RA-only variant, with the same teacher and number of trajectories, also exceeds the 28K mixture on average over the four primary benchmarks: 39.2 versus 38.7.

04Why it works

Evidence that the dependency is real

Three controlled tests carry the argument: both operations help, matching the training data's size and multi-image share does not reproduce the gain, and the trained agent depends on what its visual search tools return.

RA-only ablation 37.3 → 39.2 → 40.5

With the same teacher, backbone, 12K trajectories and SFT settings, RA alone raises the four-benchmark average by 1.9 points, and adding CE raises it by a further 1.3.

Appendix D

Composition-matched control +2.7 points on average

An open-source control drawn from the 28K mixture with RACE's 12,309 trajectories and 41.2% multi-image share stays 1.8–3.6 points below RACE on every primary benchmark.

Appendix B.3

Masking visual search returns 51.5 → 36.1 VisBrowse-Bench

Masking the returns of image search and text-to-image search, with the tool set and interaction limits unchanged, lowers RACE's accuracy from 51.5 to 36.1 on VisBrowse-Bench and from 40.2 to 35.2 on MMSearch-Plus. Masking either one alone also lowers it.

Appendix G

Eight more analysesTraining-data audit · image removal · question-only probe · required steps · tool budgets · parallel calls · visual use · variability

Training-data audit 96.2% vs. 22.3–61.8%

A judge labels 500 sampled trajectories per source. 96.2% of RACE trajectories identify the target from the image and obtain evidence for every relevant entity; 86.8% do so while verifying at least three entities.

Appendix F

Image-removal test 40.9% → 8.2%

On 499 cross-image tasks, a search probe answers 40.9% with both images and 8.2% on average when one image is withheld.

Appendix C.2

Question-only probe 2.4% revised and kept
2.7% discarded

The probe runs after reference construction and static leakage checks, and tests whether web search from the question alone can still recover a target's identity or the answer.

Appendix C.1

Required steps 21.9 → 24.1 Strict Accuracy, MM-BC

On MM-BrowseComp, Strict Accuracy, which also requires every checklist step, rises by 2.2 points, matching the overall gain; answers that are correct with steps missing stay at 9.4%. On BrowseComp-V³, subgoal completion rises from 56.0 to 62.5.

Appendix G

Tool-call budgets Ahead at every budget

With a per-question cap on tool calls, RACE stays ahead of the base agent at every budget: by 0.8–2.2 points on MM-BrowseComp and 2.7–7.0 points on BrowseComp-V³.

Appendix E

Parallel calls, fewer rounds 0.91–0.97 → 1.41–1.47 calls per round

On three of the four primary benchmarks RACE uses fewer interaction rounds than the base agent; its additional tool calls come mainly from parallel calls within a round.

Section 4.5, Appendix E

Visual search beyond the first round 34.6% of RACE training trajectories

Visual-tool use continues after the first round in 34.6% of RACE training trajectories, compared with 6.0%, 5.6% and 1.7% for OpenSearch-VL, Vision-DeepResearch and REDSearcher-MM. After SFT, the agent uses visual tools beyond the first round more often on all four primary benchmarks, by 12.5–19.5 points.

Section 4.4, Section 4.5

Variability 4 sampled runs per question · mean ± std

Every question is answered four times with the same decoding settings and turn limits; RACE has higher mean accuracy than the base agent on all four primary benchmarks.
